## Outline

**Working title:** From $0 to Six Figures a Month, One Segment at a Time
**Topic:** A marketing budget isn't one number with one split. It's a portfolio of segment experiments, each at its own stage, moving from sales-led to marketing-led top of funnel.

1. **The moment:** Walking non-marketing colleagues through why the budget can't jump from zero to six figures overnight, and how buyers move through awareness, interest, decision and action (AIDA).
2. **The situation:** $0, then upper five figures a month, then six figures a month. Binet and Field's B2B work for LinkedIn's B2B Institute links about 46% brand and 54% activation with the strongest growth. That's the destination for an established company, not a starting point.
3. **The hidden system:** Non-marketers expect marketing to work like sales: spend this quarter, get pipeline this quarter. Brand compounds over years, demand gen pays this quarter, and each target segment sits at a different stage of the funnel.
4. **What people get wrong:** That the budget is one number with one split. Starting at 0% brand starts the compounding late.
5. **The fix:** The segment glide path.
   - **Year 1 (product-market fit):** sales-led, about 20% brand and 80% demand gen, small experiments in one or two segments
   - **Year 2 (early adoption):** scale the segments that convert, brand starts making outbound cheaper
   - **Year 3 (scale):** marketing starts owning the top of the funnel, brand lowers CAC across channels
   - **Year 4 (maturity):** marketing-led, the split approaches 40/60 to 46/54
   - **Graduate a segment on signals, not the calendar:** pipeline efficiency, LTV to CAC, branded search and inbound share
6. **The close:** "At six figures a month, the question changes from 'does marketing work?' to 'which segment do we teach next?'"

**Framework:** The segment glide path (20/80 to 46/54).
