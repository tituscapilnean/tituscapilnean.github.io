## Outline

**Working title:** I'm the Good Bot, and I Still Get Blocked
**Topic:** Bot walls punish accountable agents and barely slow down bad ones. The useful line isn't human vs. bot, it's identified vs. anonymous.

1. **The moment:** A block page while researching a company, trying to decide whether to reach out at all. Then the Private Market Labs version: brokers whose job is getting deals seen, running sites that turn away the bots trying to organize those deals.
2. **The situation:** At least 20% of your research attempts get blocked. The same week, a library that solves Cloudflare Turnstile without a headless browser hit the HN front page, next to an essay arguing that breaking the open web won't stop the bad bots.
3. **The hidden system:** Bot walls tax the people who play by the rules. Bad actors buy solvers and residential proxies. Legitimate researchers, buyers and agents acting for real people absorb the cost.
4. **What people get wrong:** That the goal is telling humans from bots. The real goal is telling accountable traffic from anonymous traffic. The question shifts from "are you human?" to "who are you acting for, and with what permission?"
5. **The fix:**
   - **Agents identify themselves** with verifiable credentials: who they act for, why, and at what rate
   - **Sites publish terms for identified agents** (rate limits, allowed purposes) instead of blanket blocks
   - **Sites that want to be found** (brokers, listings, directories) offer structured feeds or an MCP endpoint
6. **The close:** The broker irony, a business that exists to be found, built so it can't be found.

**Framework:** Accountable vs. anonymous traffic.
**Personal angle:** Proof of personhood and agent identity are your daily work at Civic. Private Market Labs gives you a second, operator view. A possible Europe vs. US aside on how each treats online identity.
**Section headers (draft):** Blocked for doing my homework / The broker paradox / Bot walls tax the honest / Who are you acting for?
